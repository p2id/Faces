Supporting evidence summary:
- Clear hierarchy, restrained color, modular grouping, and direct labels consistently improve scanning.
- Mobile-first stacking and content-driven breakpoints support reliable adaptation across widths.
- Persistent labels, visible focus, semantic order, adequate targets, and non-color status cues improve accessibility.
- Summaries before details and progressive disclosure reduce perceived complexity.
- Motion is most valuable for orientation, continuity, and state change; static equivalents are required for reduced-motion preferences.
- Planning workspace provides the strongest balance of reuse, clarity, responsive composition, accessibility, and density control.
