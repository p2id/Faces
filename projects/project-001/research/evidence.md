Supporting evidence summary:
- Reusable product interfaces consistently benefit from calm surfaces, explicit hierarchy, modular grouping, and semantic status treatment.
- Primary action, current context, and next state should remain visible at each viewport.
- Accessibility risk increases with icon-only controls, color-only meaning, hidden focus, low contrast, and motion-dependent feedback.
- Responsive quality depends on priority-based reflow rather than proportional shrinking.
- Planning and scheduling provides a broad test case for time hierarchy, filtering, state transitions, overview, and detail.
- Confidence is high for structural and accessibility patterns, medium for domain selection, and low for decorative treatment before visual validation.
